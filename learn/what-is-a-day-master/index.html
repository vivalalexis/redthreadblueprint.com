What is a day master?

Of the eight characters in a BaZi or Saju chart, one is you. The rest describe what that one is standing in.

The short answer

A BaZi chart is eight characters, arranged as four pillars of two. One pillar for the year you were born, one for the month, one for the day, one for the hour. Each pillar has an upper character, called a heavenly stem, and a lower one, called an earthly branch.

Your day master is the upper character of the day pillar. In Chinese practice it is 日主 rizhu, in Korean practice 일간 ilgan. Both mean the same thing: the character that stands for you.

Why one of the eight gets to be you

The other seven characters are not lesser. They are context. The year pillar is the era and the family you arrived into, the month pillar is the season and the strongest single influence on how your day master behaves, the hour pillar is the private end of a life. Your day master is the thing all of that is happening to.

That is why a reading starts there. Without a fixed reference point, eight characters are just eight characters. With one, every other character becomes a relationship: this one supports you, this one drains you, this one is the kind of pressure you either use or get flattened by.

The ten, and what they actually are

There are ten heavenly stems, made of the five elements in two polarities each. Yang Wood and Yin Wood, Yang Fire and Yin Fire, and so on through Earth, Metal and Water.

The classical images are concrete rather than abstract. Yang Wood is a tree, Yin Wood is a vine. Both are Wood; one holds a line and the other goes around it. Neither is the stronger, because strength in a chart is not a property of the stem. It is a property of the situation the stem landed in.

What a day master does not tell you

This is the part most day master content skips.

On its own, a day master tells you almost nothing about a life. It is one character out of eight, and the season it was born into can change its reading completely. Yang Wood born in a wet spring and Yang Wood born in a dry autumn behave differently enough that the same advice would be wrong for one of them.

So a page that says “Yang Wood people are ambitious leaders” is describing a tendency stripped of every condition that governs it. The tendency is real. Read without the season, the roots and the company, it is a horoscope.

Finding yours, and what to do next

The day master comes from the date, so it takes one field to compute. What it means in your particular chart takes the rest of the chart, which is what a reading is for.

Common questions

Notes
Is a day master the same as a star sign?

No, and the difference matters more than the similarity. A sun sign is one of twelve and comes from the month. A day master is one of ten and comes from the day, then gets read against the season, the other characters and the decade you are in. Two people who share a day master and nothing else will read very differently.

Do I need my birth time to find my day master?

No. The day pillar comes from the date. A birth time sharpens everything around it, particularly the hour pillar, but the day master itself is available from the date alone.

Can my day master change?

No. It is fixed at birth and does not move. What changes is the company it keeps: the annual and decade cycles bring different elements alongside it, which is why the same chart reads differently at thirty than at fifty.
