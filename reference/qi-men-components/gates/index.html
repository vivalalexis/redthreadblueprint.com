The eight gates 八門

The action layer of a plate. Eight gates, each describing the kind of move a square supports, and three of them classically favourable.

What the eight gates are

The eight gates 八門 are the action layer of a Qi Men Dun Jia plate. Where the stars describe conditions, a gate describes the kind of move a square supports: beginning something, resting, holding a position, being seen.

Each gate has a home palace, and it carries that palace’s element. The element is derived from the seat rather than assigned separately, which is why a gate and its home always agree.

Three of the eight are classically auspicious, the 三吉門: Open, Rest and Life. Three more are read as obstructive or draining. Two sit in neither group, and the tradition leaves them there rather than forcing every gate into a column.

Every cast carries all eight

Like the stars, the gates are a permutation. Every plate contains all eight, so a reading is never asking which gates turned up. It is asking where each landed, and in particular which one fell on the palace the question is being read from.

This is also why a list of gates is not a reading. The same gate is doing something quite different depending on the palace it is sitting in and what else is sitting with it.

A name is not a verdict

The gate names include the Death gate and the Fear gate, and this practice does not soften either.

The reason is the same one that keeps 天芮 intact among the stars. A gate name is a structural label, fixed in the classical scheme, and a reading that renames it is not being kinder; it is being harder to check. So 死門 stays the Death gate, and what changed is the reading beside it: endings and stillness, the place where something is finished rather than started. Not a prediction, and not about anybody’s life.

That distinction between a name and a claim is doing real work here, and it is worth carrying into anything else you read about this system.

Common questions

Notes
Why is there a Death gate?

死門 is one of the eight classical gate names, and this practice keeps it. A gate name is a fixed structural label rather than a prediction about anyone, and renaming it would only make the plate harder to check against any other source. Its reading is endings and stillness: the place where something is finished rather than started. It is never read as a statement about a person's life.

Why are there only eight gates for nine palaces?

Because the centre palace carries no gate. That is a construction fact rather than a gap, and it is why the gates number eight where the stars number nine.

Which gates are the good ones?

Classical practice names three as 三吉門, the three auspicious gates: Open, Rest and Life. Three more are read as obstructive, and two sit in neither group. That is a starting lean rather than a verdict, and where a gate falls on the plate matters more than which gate it is.

Unsettled ground. Alarms, rumours, and news that arrives suddenly.

Open ground. Beginnings, official business, and the doors that are actually ajar.
