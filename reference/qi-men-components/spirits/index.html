The eight spirits 八神

The tone layer of a plate. Eight spirits with no fixed seat, turning with the Chief on every cast.

What the eight spirits are

The eight spirits 八神 are the third layer of a Qi Men Dun Jia plate, and the one that reads as tone. If a star is the weather and a gate is the kind of move available, a spirit is the register the whole square is operating in: cooperative, guarded, unclear, expansive.

The English name is unhelpful and worth setting aside early. Nothing about how they are read requires them to be anything other than a layer of the diagram, and this practice does not treat them as entities with intentions toward anyone.

They turn, where the others sit

This is the structural difference worth knowing. The nine stars and the eight gates each belong to a palace and carry that palace’s element. The spirits belong to none.

They rotate as a fixed set, anchored on 值符, the Chief, which itself sits with the governing star of the cast. So the spirits’ arrangement is the layer most directly tied to the particular moment, and it is the reason two plates that look similar in their stars and gates can read quite differently.

Every cast carries all eight

As with the other two layers, a plate is a permutation. All eight spirits are present on every cast, so nothing here is rare and nothing is a find. What a reading looks at is which one landed on the palace the question is being read from, and what it does to the star and gate already sitting there.

A name is not a verdict

Two of the eight have names that sound like warnings, and one has a name that sounds like a blessing. None of them is either.

This practice keeps the classical names and neutralises the readings, because a name is a structural label and a reading is a claim. So White Tiger stays White Tiger, and the line beside it describes a forceful, high friction tone: movement, distance, things that arrive hard. That is a description of ground, not of a person, and every one of these glosses is written to that rule.

Common questions

Notes
Are the eight spirits supernatural beings?

No, and the English word carries more freight than the practice does. They are one of three layers on a plate, and they function as a tone: the register a square is operating in. Nothing here asks you to believe in anything, and no reading on this site treats them as entities with intentions.

Why do the spirits have no home palace?

Because they rotate. The stars and the gates each belong to a palace and carry its element; the spirits do not. They turn with 值符, the Chief, which anchors on the governing star of the cast, so their arrangement changes with the moment in a way the other two layers do not.

What about White Tiger and Black Tortoise?

Both are classical names and both read as difficult tone rather than as omens. White Tiger reads as forceful and high friction, things arriving hard; Black Tortoise reads as unclear, partial information and dealings that go astray. Neither is a prediction, and neither is about a person.

An expansive, outward tone. Ambition, travel, and room overhead.
