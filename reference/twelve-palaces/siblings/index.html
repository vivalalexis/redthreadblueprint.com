Siblings 兄弟

xiōng dì

Siblings, close collaborators, and the give and take of equals. The palace of the people who stand beside you rather than above or below.

What the Siblings palace covers

兄弟 is the palace of equals. Brothers and sisters are the obvious case, and the subject is broader than that: it covers close collaborators, the people who work beside you rather than for or above you, and the give and take that runs between you.

The word to hold onto is level. A relationship belongs here when neither person is the one in charge.

Read it with Friends opposite it

This palace faces 僕役 Friends, the wider social field. Classical practice never reads a palace on its own, and this pairing is one of the more legible ones.

The two are the same question at two distances. Siblings covers the handful of people close enough to argue with; Friends covers the network beyond that, and the support or friction that comes from it. A chart can be busy at one distance and quiet at the other, and noticing that gap is most of what the pair is for.

Its trine palaces, Property and Health, complete the 三方四正 influence set reaching this palace.

The star supplies the tendency, the palace supplies the subject

The palace names the subject and the stars decide how it tends to go. 兄弟 with a commanding star in it reads differently from 兄弟 with a restless one, and neither is a good or bad result on its own.

That is the general rule across the twelve, and it is why a page can describe a palace honestly and still not tell you anything about your own.

When it is empty

No major star here is common and unremarkable. The tradition calls it 空宮 and reads it through the opposite palace, borrowing the stars sitting in Friends.

That borrowed reading is genuinely weaker than a star sitting in place, and a reading that treats it as equally solid is overstating what the method supports. Where a key palace is empty, the honest move is to say the read is indirect.

What is actually in yours depends on your birth hour, and the free chart will show you.

Common questions

Notes
What if I have no siblings?

The palace still reads. Its subject is the relationship of equals rather than a count of brothers and sisters, so in a chart with no siblings it speaks to close collaborators, business partners and the people you work alongside on level terms. It does not go blank.

Does this palace predict how many siblings I will have?

No, and any source promising that is overreaching. Classical practice reads the palace for the character of those relationships, the give and take in them, not for a number.

Why is it read with the Friends palace?

Because 兄弟 and 僕役 sit directly opposite each other on the square, and the tradition reads every palace with the one facing it. Close equals and the wider social field are two ends of the same subject: who stands beside you, near in and further out.

What reaches Siblings from across the chart

Computed · 三方四正

A palace is never read alone. The one directly opposite informs it, and two more reach it from across the square at a hundred and twenty degrees. Together those four cells are its influence set, and a star transformed in any of them is felt here.
